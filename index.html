A cross-city research project

Green Stormwater Infrastructure Maintenance

We are a research team from the University of Toronto's Department of Geography & Planning investigating how green stormwater infrastructure (GSI) is maintained across four North American cities.

Funded by an Insight Development Grant from the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada (SSHRC).

What is green stormwater infrastructure?

Green stormwater infrastructure (GSI) uses plants, soil, and other natural features to manage rain where it falls. Rain gardens, bioswales, stormwater tree trenches, green roofs, and permeable paving capture runoff from streets and rooftops, slow it down, and let it soak into the ground instead of rushing into sewers.

Why it matters

As cities invest in GSI to reduce flood risk and deliver multiple co-benefits, long-term success hinges on how these systems are maintained. That presents both a challenge and an opportunity — to create green jobs, strengthen community capacity, and address environmental inequities. Our research underscores the critical role of labor and equity in ensuring GSI delivers lasting benefits across all communities.

We aim to better understand GSI maintenance systems by exploring:

  • How cities partner with organizations to maintain GSI assets
  • What maintenance looks like by asset type, season, and location — and who does the work
  • How maintenance labor is resourced, valued, and whether equity or justice frameworks guide this work
  • Where gaps in coverage exist, and how these map onto social and spatial inequalities
